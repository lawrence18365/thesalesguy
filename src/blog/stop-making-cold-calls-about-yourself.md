<p>If you are cold calling today, ban the phrases that make the call all about you.</p>

<p>"We have years of experience."</p>

<p>"Our process is different."</p>

<p>"We use AI."</p>

<p>"We have a limited-time offer."</p>

<p>"We work with companies like yours."</p>

<p>"We just won an award."</p>

<p>"We are the cheapest on the market."</p>

<p>"We are a market leader."</p>

<p>See the pattern?</p>

<p>Me. Me. Me.</p>

<div class="section-divider"></div>

<h2>Your Prospect Does Not Care About Your Credentials Yet</h2>

<p>Most cold calls fail because the salesperson starts with the seller's world.</p>

<p>Their company. Their process. Their award. Their technology. Their customer base. Their special offer.</p>

<p>None of that is automatically relevant to the prospect.</p>

<p>It might become relevant later. But at the start of a cold call, the prospect is busy, guarded, and trying to work out whether this interruption deserves any attention.</p>

<p>If your first move is to talk about yourself, you give them a simple reason to switch off.</p>

<p>That is why so many calls get hit with reflex objections. The prospect hears another person trying to sell something before they have shown any understanding of the buyer's world.</p>

<h2>AI and Awards Do Not Create Relevance</h2>

<p>Salespeople love borrowing credibility from anything that sounds impressive.</p>

<p>Years in business. New technology. Big-name clients. Awards. Market-leader claims.</p>

<p>But credibility without relevance does not land.</p>

<p>If the prospect does not understand the problem you might help them solve, your credentials are just noise.</p>

<p>"We use AI" does not matter unless the prospect has a problem that AI helps solve.</p>

<p>"We are cheaper" does not matter unless price is the problem.</p>

<p>"We work with big brands" does not matter unless that proof connects to the prospect's situation.</p>

<p>Otherwise, the call sounds like a brochure being read at someone who did not ask for it.</p>

<h2>Lead With Their Problem Instead</h2>

<p>A better cold call starts with the prospect's world.</p>

<p>What is happening in their role? What problem might they be dealing with? What pressure are they under? What is slowing them down, costing them money, creating risk, or making their team less effective?</p>

<p>That is where relevance begins.</p>

<p>Instead of:</p>

<div class="article-pullquote">
    "We are a market leader with a unique process."
</div>

<p>Try to get closer to the problem:</p>

<div class="article-pullquote">
    "A lot of teams I speak to are making the calls, but the meetings are not qualified enough to convert. Is that something you are seeing, or are your reps creating proper pipeline?"
</div>

<p>Now the prospect can respond to something real.</p>

<p>They can agree, disagree, challenge it, or explain their world. That is a conversation.</p>

<p>Listing your credentials is not a conversation. It is a pitch.</p>

<h2>Earn the Right to Talk About Yourself</h2>

<p>There is a place for proof.</p>

<p>There is a place for your process, your clients, your results, and why you are different.</p>

<p>But you earn that place by first making the call relevant to the prospect.</p>

<p>Find out whether the problem exists. Ask useful questions. Listen. Understand the commercial impact. Then, if there is a reason to continue, your proof has context.</p>

<p>This connects directly to why <a href="/blog/qualified-b2b-sales-conversations-start-with-problem/">qualified B2B sales conversations start with a problem</a>. The buyer does not care about what you do until they care about why it matters to them.</p>

<p>Stop making cold calls about yourself.</p>

<p>Make them about the prospect's problem.</p>
