<p>"Your target for the month is 15 meetings. Get on the phone and make it happen."</p>

<p>This happens in sales teams everywhere.</p>

<p>A young salesperson gets handed a target, a phone, a CRM, and a few days of product knowledge. Then everyone acts surprised when the calls sound desperate.</p>

<p>That is not sales leadership. That is a hit-and-hope strategy.</p>

<div class="section-divider"></div>

<h2>Meeting Targets Do Not Teach SDRs How to Sell</h2>

<p>Training in many sales organisations is little more than a product dump.</p>

<p>Slide after slide of features, benefits, customer logos, testimonials, and internal language. Then the rep is expected to throw all of that at anyone who answers the phone.</p>

<p>When it fails, blame gets placed on the salesperson.</p>

<p>But what did you actually teach them?</p>

<p>You taught them what the product does. You did not teach them how to create a business conversation with a stranger.</p>

<p>You did not teach them how to lead with problems, ask useful questions, handle reflex objections, control their tone, or qualify properly.</p>

<p>Then you measured them on meetings and wondered why they started begging for calendar invites.</p>

<h2>Prospecting Needs Small Wins</h2>

<p>Cold calling is tough, especially for people early in their sales career.</p>

<p>Throwing a meeting target at a new rep without coaching is like asking someone to climb Mount Everest as their first hike. The goal is too far away to be useful.</p>

<p>Constant rejection makes the salesperson feel like they are failing. When every day is judged only by whether a meeting got booked, most of the learning gets ignored.</p>

<p>That is why managers need to measure small wins as well as outcomes.</p>

<ul>
    <li>Is the pickup-to-conversation ratio improving?</li>
    <li>Are they opening with problems instead of features?</li>
    <li>Are they taking feedback and applying it on the next call?</li>
    <li>Are they getting better at dealing with reflex objections?</li>
    <li>Is the conversation-to-positive-outcome rate improving?</li>
    <li>Are they asking deeper and more thoughtful questions?</li>
</ul>

<p>Those are the signals that a salesperson is improving.</p>

<p>Small wins eventually become meetings. But if you only measure the meeting, you miss the development that creates it.</p>

<h2>Sales Leaders Need to Listen to the Calls</h2>

<p>Look at the numbers, not just the calls your reps tell you about.</p>

<p>Listen to the recordings. Sit beside them. Make dials in front of them. Show them what good sounds like.</p>

<p>The reason this does not happen enough is uncomfortable: many sales leaders have not made a cold call in years.</p>

<p>Some have never made one properly.</p>

<p>So instead of coaching the call, they manage the dashboard. They demand meetings from the sidelines because it is easier than showing the team how to get them.</p>

<p>That is not enough.</p>

<p>If you want your SDRs to improve, you need to coach the behaviour that creates the result. Meeting targets matter, but they are the outcome. They are not the training plan.</p>
