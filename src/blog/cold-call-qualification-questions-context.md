<p>"You cannot possibly ask that many questions on a cold call, especially with a C-suite prospect."</p>

<p>I hear this a lot when I teach qualification.</p>

<p>When I ask why, the answer is usually some version of this:</p>

<p>"Strangers do not like being interrogated."</p>

<p>That is true.</p>

<p>It is also slightly off.</p>

<div class="section-divider"></div>

<h2>Prospects Do Not Resist Questions</h2>

<p>Prospects do not resist questions.</p>

<p>They resist badly asked questions.</p>

<p>The issue is not always the number of questions. The issue is how disconnected those questions feel.</p>

<p>Most reps fall into this pattern:</p>

<ul>
    <li>"What is the impact?"</li>
    <li>"Why does that matter?"</li>
    <li>"Can you give me an example?"</li>
</ul>

<p>Individually, those are fine questions.</p>

<p>Together, asked without context, they feel like a checklist.</p>

<p>No flow. No sign the salesperson has actually been listening. No reason for the prospect to believe the next question is connected to the last answer.</p>

<p>So the prospect feels processed, not understood.</p>

<p>That is when resistance shows up.</p>

<h2>Build Into the Question</h2>

<p>The better approach is simple.</p>

<p>Do not jump straight to the next question. Build into it.</p>

<p>Reflect what the prospect has said. Tighten it. Then ask the next question.</p>

<p>For example:</p>

<div class="article-pullquote">
    "You mentioned the current process is not really working. Where is it breaking down most often?"
</div>

<div class="article-pullquote">
    "When you say doing better, what specifically is not happening today?"
</div>

<div class="article-pullquote">
    "What does a typical call sound like when it does not land well?"
</div>

<p>The intent is the same. You are still qualifying. You are still trying to understand the problem, the impact, and whether it is worth fixing.</p>

<p>But the experience for the buyer is completely different.</p>

<p>Now the questions do not feel random. They feel earned.</p>

<h2>Listening Gives You Permission to Go Deeper</h2>

<p>Cold call qualification is not about firing questions until the prospect gives up.</p>

<p>It is about showing enough understanding that the next question makes sense.</p>

<p>If you sound like you have been listening, you can ask far more than most salespeople think. If you sound like you are reading from a qualification checklist, even one simple question can feel annoying.</p>

<p>This is especially true when speaking with senior people.</p>

<p>C-suite prospects do not mind direct questions. In fact, many prefer them. What they do not tolerate is wasting time with a salesperson who asks generic questions that could have been asked of anyone.</p>

<p>Specificity matters. Context matters. Flow matters.</p>

<h2>Qualification Should Feel Like a Conversation</h2>

<p>The goal is not to interrogate the prospect.</p>

<p>The goal is to understand whether a real problem exists and whether there is a business reason to keep talking.</p>

<p>That requires questions. But those questions need to come from the conversation, not from a script the salesperson is desperate to complete.</p>

<p>Prospects mind being asked questions by someone who does not sound like they have been listening.</p>

<p>If it feels like you understand their world, you can ask almost anything.</p>
