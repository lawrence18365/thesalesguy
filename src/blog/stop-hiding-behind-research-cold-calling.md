<p>Yesterday I spent 45 minutes researching a prospect.</p>

<p>They were perfect.</p>

<p>They ran a sales team.</p>

<p>The team was new.</p>

<p>They had little cold-calling experience.</p>

<p>They had just received funding.</p>

<p>The decision-maker was from my hometown.</p>

<p>We supported the same football team.</p>

<p>We even had mutual connections.</p>

<p>Rapport was going to be easy.</p>

<p>I was already spending the commission in my head.</p>

<p>After 45 minutes of research, I finally picked up the phone.</p>

<p>What happened next?</p>

<p>It went straight to voicemail.</p>

<div class="section-divider"></div>

<h2>Research Feels Productive Before a Cold Call</h2>

<p>That is the trap.</p>

<p>Research feels like work. It feels professional. It feels like preparation. It gives you something to do that looks sensible from the outside.</p>

<p>But if you spend 45 minutes researching one prospect and never actually speak to them, you have not created an opportunity.</p>

<p>You have created a story in your head.</p>

<p>You imagine the perfect opener. You imagine the shared football team creating rapport. You imagine the funding announcement proving the timing is right. You imagine the decision-maker loving the call because you have done your homework.</p>

<p>Then the call goes to voicemail.</p>

<p>That is why endless sales research is often just <a href="/blog/overcome-call-reluctance/">call reluctance</a> wearing a better suit.</p>

<h2>Most Salespeople Do Not Have a Lead Problem</h2>

<div class="article-pullquote">
    "Most salespeople don't have a lead problem. They have an activity problem."
</div>

<p>Salespeople love blaming the list.</p>

<p>The data is wrong. The prospect is not right. The industry is difficult. The timing is off. The person is too senior. The company is too small. The company is too big.</p>

<p>Sometimes the list is bad.</p>

<p>Most of the time, it is fine.</p>

<p>The real issue is that the salesperson is trying to remove every ounce of uncertainty before picking up the phone.</p>

<p>That will never happen.</p>

<p>You can know the prospect's job title, company size, funding history, mutual connections, team structure, favourite football team, and hometown.</p>

<p>You still do not know whether they will answer.</p>

<p>That only happens when you call.</p>

<h2>Preparation Should Make the Call Better, Not Stop It Happening</h2>

<p>Research is not useless.</p>

<p>Bad research is.</p>

<p>The point of research is to make the call sharper. It should help you understand the likely problem, the role, the business context, and why the conversation might matter.</p>

<p>It should not become a hiding place.</p>

<p>If you need 45 minutes before every call, you are not prospecting. You are writing a screenplay for a conversation that may never happen.</p>

<p>This is the same mistake sales teams make when they obsess over <a href="/blog/cold-call-openers-are-the-easy-part/">cold call openers</a>. They keep polishing the first sentence because polishing feels safer than dialling.</p>

<p>The best cold callers do enough research to be relevant, then they get moving.</p>

<h2>Pick Up the Phone</h2>

<p>At some point, the only useful action left is the call.</p>

<p>Not another tab.</p>

<p>Not another LinkedIn scroll.</p>

<p>Not another note in the CRM.</p>

<p>The call.</p>

<p>If they answer, you have a conversation.</p>

<p>If they do not, you move on.</p>

<p>Either way, you are now dealing with reality instead of the version of the prospect you built in your head.</p>

<p>That is why <a href="/blog/two-ways-to-start-a-cold-calling-day/">the way you start a cold calling day matters</a>. You can either build momentum or build excuses.</p>

<p>Stop hiding behind research.</p>

<p>Pick up the phone.</p>
