<p>It's not hard to book a meeting via a cold call.</p>

<p>So many people post their metrics each day, announcing the number of meetings as if that is the final metric.</p>

<p>It is not.</p>

<p>You can beg and plead your way into a prospect's diary, but if the lead is not qualified, what really is the point?</p>

<div class="section-divider"></div>

<h2>A Cold Call Meeting Is Not the Same as a Qualified Meeting</h2>

<p>A meeting on its own is just a calendar invite.</p>

<p>It might look good in the CRM. It might make the SDR dashboard look tidy. It might give leadership something to point at in the boardroom.</p>

<p>But that does not make it pipeline.</p>

<p>If the prospect does not have a real problem, does not care about fixing it, and does not have the authority or influence to move anything forward, you have not created an opportunity.</p>

<p>You have created hope.</p>

<p>Hope the prospect turns up. Hope they enjoy your pitch. Hope they buy at the end.</p>

<p>That is not a sales process. That is hit and hope.</p>

<h2>The Questions Before the Meeting Matters</h2>

<p>Your job as a cold caller is not just to book meetings.</p>

<p>Your job is to book qualified meetings.</p>

<p>Before that invite goes into the calendar, you should know:</p>

<ul>
    <li>Are you meeting a decision maker or a mid-level manager with no authority?</li>
    <li>Does the prospect actually have a problem, or have you talked them into a "review" session?</li>
    <li>If they have a problem, do they care about fixing it?</li>
    <li>What is the impact of the problem to them individually, and as a business?</li>
    <li>What have they done to actually fix the problem?</li>
    <li>Has the prospect shown any emotion, or just spoken intellectually?</li>
</ul>

<p>These are not complicated questions.</p>

<p>They are the difference between a meeting with a reason and a placeholder waiting to be cancelled.</p>

<p>This is why <a href="/blog/cold-call-qualification-questions-context/">cold call qualification questions need context</a>. You cannot just fire them out like a checklist and expect the prospect to care. But you do need the answers.</p>

<h2>Comparison Meetings Are Usually Free Consulting</h2>

<p>A "comparison" meeting comparing your solution to what they are currently using is often just a free consultation.</p>

<p>The prospect gets ideas. You get a polite thank you. Then they stay where they are.</p>

<p>Why?</p>

<p>Because no one uncovered whether the current way of working was actually painful enough to change.</p>

<p>Too many salespeople hear mild interest and mistake it for intent. They hear "happy to take a look" and treat it like pipeline.</p>

<p>It is not pipeline until there is a problem.</p>

<p>It is not pipeline until the problem has impact.</p>

<p>It is not pipeline until the prospect can explain why doing nothing is a bad idea.</p>

<h2>Meetings Do Not Pay the Bills</h2>

<div class="article-pullquote">
    "Meetings don't matter. Revenue does."
</div>

<p>Booking loads of meetings might look great on your CRM and in the boardroom.</p>

<p>But if those meetings do not convert, you have just moved the problem from the SDR to the AE.</p>

<p>Now the AE is spending their week with people who have no authority, no urgency, no emotion, and no business reason to change.</p>

<p>Then everyone calls it a closing problem.</p>

<p>It is not.</p>

<p>It is a <a href="/blog/closing-problem-qualification-problem/">qualification problem</a>.</p>

<p>Focus on the right prospects. Not all your prospects.</p>
