<p>If you run a sales team that can survive on inbound leads, getting consultants to pick up the phone can be difficult.</p>

<p>I see the same mistake all the time.</p>

<p>"If you added another ten clients a month, you could earn an extra $50k a year."</p>

<p>It sounds logical.</p>

<p>It rarely motivates people.</p>

<div class="section-divider"></div>

<h2>More Money Is Not Always the Best Motivator</h2>

<p>Sales leaders often assume every salesperson is driven by the same thing.</p>

<p>More commission. More bonus. More upside.</p>

<p>Some are. Many are not.</p>

<p>Plenty of salespeople are comfortable. They are earning enough. They have inbound leads coming through. They can tell themselves they are busy without ever having to create demand from scratch.</p>

<p>So when you tell them they could earn more by cold calling, it does not always land.</p>

<p>The promise of extra money is a gain. And gains are not always enough to change behaviour.</p>

<h2>Loss Aversion Hits Harder</h2>

<p>People are often more motivated to avoid losses than they are to chase gains.</p>

<p>That is loss aversion.</p>

<p>In sales, the cost of inaction can be more powerful than the potential upside.</p>

<p>So instead of only asking what the salesperson could gain by learning outbound, ask what they risk by avoiding it.</p>

<ul>
    <li>They become order takers who wait for marketing to feed them.</li>
    <li>They rely on inbound leads that may disappear when market conditions change.</li>
    <li>They limit their career options to companies with strong marketing engines.</li>
    <li>They miss the communication skills that improve every area of selling.</li>
    <li>They struggle to launch their own business because they cannot create demand.</li>
</ul>

<p>That is the real risk.</p>

<p>Not missing a bonus. Losing control of their career.</p>

<h2>The Best Salespeople Create Opportunities</h2>

<p>The best salespeople know how to create opportunities, not just respond to them.</p>

<p>Inbound is useful. It has a place. But if your entire sales function depends on people already wanting to speak to you, the market is in control.</p>

<p>When leads slow down, your team is exposed.</p>

<p>When marketing has a bad quarter, your pipeline disappears.</p>

<p>When buyers become more cautious, order takers struggle.</p>

<p>Outbound skill protects a salesperson from that. It gives them the ability to find problems, create conversations, and build pipeline even when the easy leads are not there.</p>

<p>That is why I wrote about the danger of relying on inbound in <a href="/blog/inbound-vs-outbound-sales-control/">Why Relying on Inbound Leads Is Killing Your Sales</a>. Inbound can support sales. It should not own it.</p>

<h2>Focus on What They Stand to Lose</h2>

<p>Next time you want your team to pick up the phone, do not only focus on what they could gain.</p>

<p>Focus on what they stand to lose.</p>

<p>Because if they never learn outbound, they are not just missing meetings. They are handing control of their career, income, and options to someone else.</p>
